## The method (as taught in the episode)
The approach is **metakognitiv terapi** (metacognitive therapy): change how you relate to your thoughts, not their content.
1. **Know the types.** *Bekymring* = "what-if" thoughts about the future; *grubling* = "why" thoughts about the past; *tankekjør* = the same negative thought replaying until it takes over.
2. **Catch yourself.** Most of the time we don't notice we're worrying. «Ta deg selv i å tenke negativt.»
3. **Acknowledge and leave the thoughts alone.** Don't dig into them and don't push them away; both give them more attention and tell the brain they're dangerous, which strengthens them. «Anerkjenn at tankene er der, og bytt tema.»
4. **Ask whether you can act now.** If there's nothing you can do about it "der og da", you don't need to do anything; solving it in your head only creates stress. Thoughts are not dangerous: «Tanker er bare tanker.»
5. **Practise detached attention (frakoblet oppmerksomhet).** Notice thoughts, feelings and body sensations, let them appear and pass, observe without judging and without engaging.
6. **Practise flexible attention (fleksibel oppmerksomhet).** Shift focus between outside sounds or sights (birds, cars); describing what you see or hear works even better. The goal is to move attention from inside your head out into the world.
7. **Don't make big decisions in the middle of the feeling** (the "fly to Thailand" example).
8. **Self-esteem:** don't force positive self-talk; instead stop keeping it low. Stop comparing yourself, scrutinising yourself for faults and replaying embarrassing moments. Notice negative self-talk and let it go. It can take a long time.

**Takeaways (20:07):** 1) Du er ikke tankene dine. 2) De fleste problemer blir ikke mindre av å tenke, gruble og bekymre seg. 3) Life is complicated and strange; don't take everything so seriously, enjoy what you do, you deserve to feel good.

## Grammar points with real lines
1. **Reflexive verbs** (*bekymre seg, grue seg, forholde seg, kjenne seg igjen*). The pronoun changes with the subject:
   - «Jeg bekymrer **meg** mest for å bli syk.» / «Er du en som bekymrer **deg**?» / «…hvordan man forholder **seg** til tanker.»
2. **burde + perfect participle = "should have"**
   - «…denne metoden **burde vært** pensum.» / «Jeg **burde vært** smartere.» / «…at jeg **burde gjort** noe mer med livet mitt.» (Spoken Norwegian often drops *ha*: *burde (ha) vært*.)
3. **Inversion (V2) after a fronted element**: the verb comes second.
   - «**Heldigvis kan man** lære seg…» / «**Først har vi** bekymringer.» / «**Nå skal jeg** fortelle deg hvordan.»
4. **Imperatives** for instructions:
   - «**Ta** deg selv i å tenke negativt, **anerkjenn** at tankene er der, og **bytt** tema.» / «**Observer** dem uten å dømme.» / «**La** tankene dukke opp.»
5. **jo … jo** ("the more … the …"):
   - «For **jo mer** du graver i disse tankene, **jo verre** blir det.»
6. **kommer til å + infinitive** (future/prediction):
   - «Da **kommer** drømmen min **til å** bli helt ødelagt.»

## Comprehension questions
1. What is the difference between *bekymring* and *grubling*? → Bekymring is about the future ("what if…"); grubling is about the past ("why did I…").
2. Why shouldn't you push negative thoughts away? → It gives them more attention and signals to the brain that they're dangerous, so they get stronger and come back more.
3. What should you do if there's nothing you can do about a problem "der og da"? → Nothing; leave the thoughts and don't spend time and energy on them.
4. How does Dennis practise *fleksibel oppmerksomhet* outdoors? → He moves his focus to birds, cars or sounds around him, and it works even better if he describes them.
5. According to metacognitive therapy, how do you improve self-esteem? → Not by building it with positive self-talk, but by stopping the habits that keep it low (comparing, self-scrutiny, replaying embarrassments).

## Speaking / writing prompt
Skriv eller si 5–8 setninger: **«Hva gjør du når du får tankekjør?»** Use at least: *bekymre seg for*, *dukke opp*, *la tankene være*, one *burde vært*, and one *jo … jo*. Tip for Tromsø: try *fleksibel oppmerksomhet* outside. Describe what you hear and see (*Jeg hører måkene… Jeg ser fjellet…*).
